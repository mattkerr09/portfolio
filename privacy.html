Privacy

Short, because the site does very little.

What this site loads from someone else

Exactly one thing: Plausible Analytics, which counts page views. It is hosted in the EU, sets no cookies, and does not build a profile of you or follow you to other sites. It records the page you viewed, the referring site, and a coarse country, device and browser — never an identifier that persists between visits.

Your IP address reaches Plausible's servers as part of any web request, and Plausible discards it after generating a daily, one-way hash used only to tell one visit from another on the same day. Nothing here can tell who you are.

Added 2026-08-14, and worth saying why. This page did not exist until analytics did. The site previously loaded nothing from any third party, so there was nothing to disclose — a check in my own tooling exempted it on exactly that basis, and adding Plausible made the exemption false. The check caught it within the hour. That is the only honest reason a privacy page appears the same day as a tracker.

What this site does not do

  • No cookies of any kind, from me or from Plausible.
  • No advertising, retargeting or conversion pixels — no Meta, Google Ads or LinkedIn tags.
  • No fonts, scripts, images or embeds from any other domain. Everything else is served from matthewkerr.dev.
  • No forms, no accounts, no newsletter. There is nothing here to submit.

If you email me

The address on this site is matthew@kerrandcompanyholdings.com, which is Google Workspace. If you write to me, Google handles that mail and I keep the message. I do not add anyone to a list.

Hosting

The site is static and served by GitHub Pages. GitHub receives the request and therefore your IP address, as any web host does, and states its handling in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Asking about your data

There is no account to delete and no profile to export, because none is created. If you want the analytics record for a visit removed, email the address above and say roughly when you visited — Plausible stores no identifier, so that is genuinely the only way to find it, and it may not be findable at all.
